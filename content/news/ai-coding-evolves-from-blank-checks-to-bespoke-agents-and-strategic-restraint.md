## The blank-check AI coding era is dead. Here’s what comes next.
![The blank-check AI coding era is dead. Here’s what comes next.](https://cdn.thenewstack.io/media/2026/03/f6ed036e-ubaid-e-alyafizi-f1n95dhtrpg-unsplash-scaled.jpg)
The initial phase of AI coding, characterized by unrestrained adoption and often unmeasured consumption of AI resources, is reportedly coming to an end. This 'blank-check' era saw developers freely utilizing AI code generation tools without a keen eye on the underlying costs or the strategic implications of token usage. However, as AI tools become more integrated and their operational expenses become clearer, a shift towards more disciplined and cost-conscious **AI operations** (AIOps) is emerging.

This transition signals a move from experimental AI use to more mature, production-grade integration where efficiency and value are paramount. Companies are now focusing on optimizing token budgets and ensuring that AI-generated code directly contributes to business objectives, rather than just increasing output. The future of AI coding will likely involve more sophisticated management tools and practices to govern AI resource consumption and measure ROI effectively.

> The industry is transitioning from unbridled AI adoption to a more strategic, cost-aware approach for AI coding, emphasizing optimized **token budgets** and measurable value.

## Why Todoist says less AI can deliver more
![Why Todoist says less AI can deliver more](https://cdn.thenewstack.io/media/2026/08/22dc6062-doist-cto-gonçalo-silva.png)
In a counter-intuitive take, **Todoist**, the popular task management application, argues that a more judicious application of AI can yield greater benefits than a pervasive, 'AI-everywhere' strategy. **Todoist**'s approach focuses on implementing AI only where it genuinely solves user problems or significantly enhances existing features, rather than integrating AI for its own sake. This philosophy prioritizes thoughtful design and user experience over simply adding AI capabilities.

The company believes that excessive AI can lead to complexity and diminish the core utility of a product. By carefully selecting where and how AI is used, **Todoist** aims to deliver impactful, seamless automation that complements human decision-making without overwhelming users. This measured strategy underscores a growing realization that not all problems require an AI solution, and sometimes, less truly is more.

> **Todoist** advocates for a focused AI strategy, deploying AI only for high-impact, user-centric automations rather than ubiquitous integration.

## Meta enters the AI coding wars with Muse Spark 1.2 and ...
![Meta enters the AI coding wars with Muse Spark 1.2 and ...](https://images.ctfassets.net/jdtwqhzvc2n1/6O04LNAC5tze3POrf1sJIb/f662aa9a3b5329ec7d56034dc95a8fdf/ChatGPT_Image_Aug_5__2026__04_43_41_PM.png?w=800&q=75)
**Meta** has made a significant move into the competitive AI coding agent market with the release of **Muse Spark 1.2** and **Muse Code**. **Muse Code** is a terminal-based AI coding agent, currently in beta, designed to handle complete software engineering tasks, from planning changes and writing code to validating results across large repositories. This launch pits **Meta** directly against established players like **Anthropic**'s **Claude Code** and **OpenAI**'s **Codex**, as well as various agentic coding harnesses.

**Meta** co-founder and CEO **Mark Zuckerberg** announced **Muse Code** on **X** (formerly **Twitter**), highlighting its capability to manage end-to-end software engineering workflows. Unlike **Meta**'s previous focus on open-weight models like **Llama**, **Muse Code** and its underlying **Muse Spark** models are entirely proprietary. However, **Zuckerberg** hinted at potential future open-source initiatives for **Muse Spark** or related products. Users can install **Muse Code** via a single `curl` command on **macOS** or **Linux**, though it requires a **Meta** account and billing details for access.

> **Meta**'s new proprietary **Muse Code** and **Muse Spark 1.2** mark its serious entry into the AI coding agent market, directly challenging existing solutions with a full-stack, terminal-based approach.

## Every software company will become a dev tools company - The New Stack
![Every software company will become a dev tools company - The New Stack](https://cdn.thenewstack.io/media/2026/08/d93cda3b-a-c-hcagvymwqsg-unsplash.jpg)
The landscape of software development is evolving, suggesting that virtually every software company is poised to become a developer tools company, at least internally. This trend is driven by the increasing complexity of modern software ecosystems and the need for tailored solutions to enhance developer productivity and efficiency within organizations. The shift towards **platform engineering** is a key accelerator of this movement, as companies invest in building robust internal platforms that provide developers with streamlined workflows and self-service capabilities.

This internal tooling revolution means that companies are no longer just consumers of third-party development tools but are actively creating bespoke solutions to address their unique challenges and optimize their specific development processes. This approach fosters greater control over the development environment, reduces dependencies, and ultimately leads to faster innovation and deployment cycles. The focus is on empowering developers with the best possible tools, whether custom-built or integrated, to achieve organizational goals.

> The era of **platform engineering** is transforming software companies into internal developer tool providers, emphasizing bespoke solutions for enhanced productivity and efficiency.
